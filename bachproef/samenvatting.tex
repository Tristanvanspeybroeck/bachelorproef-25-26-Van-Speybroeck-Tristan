\chapter*{\IfLanguageName{dutch}{Samenvatting}{Abstract}}

This research investigates to what extent an AI-based system using markerless video analysis can generate technically useful feedback for recreational gymnasts performing the kip on bars. In recreational gymnastics clubs, coaches often supervise several gymnasts at the same time, which means that technical errors are not always systematically noticed. At the same time, targeted feedback is important for learning complex skills safely and efficiently.

The aim of this research is to develop and exploratively evaluate a proof-of-concept that converts standard video footage into pose data, calculates relevant joint angles from this data, detects predefined technical errors using rule-based logic, and translates these detections into textual feedback through a Large Language Model. The focus is therefore not on a fully validated coaching system, but on the technical feasibility and limitations of such an analysis pipeline.

The proof-of-concept was built in several steps. First, MediaPipe Pose Landmarker was used to extract body landmarks from video footage. Then, based on these landmarks, the hip angle, shoulder angle, trunk angle and elbow angle were calculated and stored in CSV format. After that, a Python script was developed that processes a manually selected analysis window, detects critical anchor points, divides the movement into phases and analyses four predefined error types: bent arms, insufficient hip closure, early hip opening and weak shoulder push in the support phase. The analysis output was stored in a structured JSON format and used in a filtered form as input for a local LLM through Ollama.

The results show that markerless pose estimation can produce usable movement data from standard video footage, but that the quality remains dependent on the recording. Background movements, temporarily less visible body parts and detection noise can cause abrupt deviations in the calculated angle curves. For this reason, the analysis was refined iteratively, among other things by applying smoothing and adjusting error rules to reduce incorrect detections.

The final analysis was tested on three attempts: one good execution and two incorrect executions. In the good execution, no errors were detected. In the first incorrect execution, bent arms during the pull phase and weak shoulder push in the support phase were detected. In the second incorrect execution, mainly weak shoulder push in the support phase was detected. The results show that the rule-based approach can recognise relevant differences between attempts within the limited test set, but should not be considered a formal validation.

The LLM feedback proved technically feasible, but not fully reliable without strict delimitation. When the language model received too much information, there was a risk that it would formulate non-detected errors or general improvement points. Therefore, the LLM input was limited to the errors that were actually detected, and no LLM call is performed when the rule-based system detects no errors.

This research shows that automatic technical feedback on the kip on bars is feasible to a limited extent through a combination of markerless pose estimation, rule-based error detection and LLM-based text generation. The approach offers potential as a supporting tool for coaches, but does not replace human expertise. Further evaluation on a larger dataset and with feedback from experienced coaches remains necessary.
